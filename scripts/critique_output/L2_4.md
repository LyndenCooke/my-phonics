### 2. Narrative critique
- The core story arc is clear from text alone: child at a fair wins a pair of toy ducks, wind/air whisks them into a fir tree, a man helps, happy ending.  
- Image–text alignment is mostly solid (fair, pair of ducks, fir tree, chair).  
- Repetition of “pair” works to support the air grapheme but occasionally becomes padding.  
- Several lines sound unnatural or adult-unnatural: “such a gush”, “The air is in my hair”, “The sir said”, “The fair is so big”. These reduce read-aloud quality.  
- Dialogue punctuation sometimes makes sentences long and fussy for L2.

### 3. Level appropriateness
- The current draft breaks the “2 sentences per page” rule.  
- It does use earlier GPCs well (sh, oo, ow, ee, ay) and foregrounds air/ir, but includes a few out-of-scope items (“so”, “By”).  
- Overall length likely exceeds target due to extra sentences.

### 4. Proposed rewrite
Two sentences per page. Four to seven words each. All words restricted to the cumulative graphemes and tricky words. Natural, image-aligned, and foregrounding air/ir.

Page 1
- I go to the fair.  
- The air is cool.

Page 2
- The air is in my hair.  
- I put my hat on.

Page 3
- Look at the toy ducks.  
- I can win a pair.

Page 4
- Yes, I win the pair.  
- I hug the pair.

Page 5
- The air bumps my pair.  
- My pair shoots up, up, up!

Page 6
- Sir! Sir! My pair is up!  
- He can see it at the fir.

Page 7
- The man ran to the fir.  
- He got my pair down.

Page 8
- I sit in a chair.  
- My pair is back.

Estimated words: 84 (within 80–130 target).

### 5. Notes for the human editor
- I retained “sir” as the child’s call for help, but avoided “The sir …” as a subject. Page 7 uses “The man …” which fits the image.  
- I replaced all instances of “so” and “By” to stay within the allowed set.  
- I avoided mentioning “ladder” and “tree” (initial blends tr, and er) even though shown in the art.  
- “The air is in my hair” is kept to feature air/hair; it’s slightly stylised but acceptable as child-talk and matches the image. If you prefer, an option is “My hair is in the air,” which is equally decodable and perhaps snappier.  
- Page 5 uses “bumps” to describe the gust without using forbidden clusters or the word “of.” If you want a stronger sense of wind while keeping air/ir, an allowed alternative is “A big air push,” but it is less natural.
