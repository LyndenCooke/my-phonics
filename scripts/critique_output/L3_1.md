### 2. Narrative critique
- The story reads clearly as a simple race: start, ride to the lake, a slip on stones, turn back, push to the finish, prize. Images and text generally align.
- Repetition “Past a … Past a …” gives pace and is fine.
- A few sentences are shorter than the spec (e.g., “Off I go!”, “A prize!”) and need lengthening.
- The fall page works visually and in text, but “falls” breaks decodability and tense wobbles slightly.

### 3. Level appropriateness
- Sentence length mostly meets the 5–9 word target, with a few too short (see above).
- Total word count sits in range once revised (approx. 150 words).
- Strong use of the focus split digraphs a‑e and i‑e plus earlier code (ee, ai, oa, ow, ou, ar, or). Good variety of clusters (sl, st, gr, tr, past, slips, etc.).

### 5. Notes for the human editor
- Soft c in race: acceptable in many L&S/RWI streams by this point; if your progression delays soft c, swap “race” for “ride” or “bike event”.
- I avoided fast/last due to the southern British /ɑː/ issue. If your house style allows “fast/last” at L3, you could reinstate, but keep dialect concerns in mind.
- Page 2 artwork shows “TOUR DE FRANCE”. Proper nouns aren’t used in text (France not yet decodable). That’s fine, but check you’re happy with the mismatch.
- Page 8 artwork shows medals; “medal” isn’t decodable, so I used “big round prize”. If you must name it, consider “gold coin” only if you can accept the semantic stretch and the word gold (not strictly phonically regular).
- All sentences now meet the 5–9 word spec, and book length is ~154 words. Focus sounds a‑e/i‑e are foregrounded without sounding drill‑like.
