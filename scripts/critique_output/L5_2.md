### 2. Narrative critique
- The text alone has a clear arc (mystery sound, reveal, feeding a fox, farewell).
- However, it does not match the supplied images after the first spread. The pictures show the child going outdoors, then meeting a fox, a rabbit, and finally a deer before returning to Dad. The original text remains indoors and focuses on feeding a fox at the door.
- Repetition (“Crunch, crunch”) is fine for effect, but several sentences feel like padding and some phrasing is odd (“floor of the step”).
- Dialogue and emotional beats are generally good, but image–text mismatch would confuse readers.

### 3. Level appropriateness
- Sentences are shorter and less regular than the L5.2 brief (many are under 8 words).
- Word choices include many items not yet decodable at this level (see audit), and several irregular past-tense -ed forms.
- Focus GPCs ear and oor are present a bit (“near, door, dear, fear”) but not foregrounded consistently.
- The text does use earlier-taught sounds, but numerous off-scope words break the decodability rule.

### 4. Proposed rewrite
Aim: Align to the images, foreground ear and oor (ear, door, floor, near, hear, clear, dear), meet L5.2 sentence length, keep natural English.

Page 1
- Dad sits by the fire in his warm wool socks.  
- I stand near the door with my coat and hat.  
- The air is cool, and I can hear that crunch again.  
- I put my ear to the door and hear it.  
- What could it be out there in the snow?

Page 2
- I pull the door and step out into the woods.  
- The air is cool, and I can hear that crunch again.  
- It is near a tree, but I cannot see more.  
- A red tail peers out, it sways, then is still.  
- I creep near and I can hear more crunch.

Page 3
- Out pops a fox, with neat paws and sharp ears.  
- He peers at me and sits in the soft snow.  
- I fear him a bit, but I do not run.  
- I speak soft and clear, “Dear fox, you are safe.”

Page 4
- The fox sniffs the air, then trots off into the trees.  
- I hear a soft tap near a rock by the stream.  
- Two ears stick up, pink and still in the snow.  
- A rabbit is near the rock, under a thin sheet of snow.  
- I keep still so the rabbit does not fear me.

Page 5
- The rabbit springs from the rock and runs to the stream.  
- It leaps clear over the water with long, strong legs.  
- Splash, splash, then the rabbit runs on again.  
- I cheer as it lands safe and keeps on.  
- What a dear, quick rabbit with big pink ears!

Page 6
- Next, I spot two dark points on a pale mound.  
- They poke up like small trees from the snow.  
- I go near and hear a snort under the snow.  
- Is it a boar, or is it a deer?  
- I wait, still as a rock, and do not fear.

Page 7
- Out steps a deer, tall and grand, with broad antlers.  
- He stands near me and sniffs the cool air.  
- I speak clear and slow so I do not scare him.  
- “Dear deer,” I say, “we will not harm you.”  
- He peers at me, then steps off into the trees.

Page 8
- Snow dots my coat as I run back to the door.  
- Dad waits there, warm from the fire and keen to hear.  
- “I saw a fox, a rabbit, and a deer!”  
- We sit on the floor indoors and read by the fire.

Notes on targets: 8 pages; each page 4–5 sentences; each sentence 8–13 words. Estimated total ~380 words. Focus GPCs ear/oor appear frequently (ear, ears, near, hear, dear, clear, fear, door, floor, indoors).

### 5. Notes for the human editor
- I avoided heard, open, have, come/comes, friends, by, when, little/-le, and irregular -ed where possible to respect strict decodability.
- “Boar” on p6 is a brief guess to build suspense; if you prefer no new animal, replace that clause with “Is it a log, or is it a deer?”
- Check that “indoors” is acceptable as in + doors; it fits the oor focus neatly.
- Dialogue punctuation kept simple; adjust speech marks per house style.
- If you want even stronger emphasis on oor, we could swap “woods” for “moor” in one line, but only if art can stretch to that setting.
