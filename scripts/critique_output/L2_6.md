### 2. Narrative critique
- The sequence (arrive at night market → look at lamps → hear music → eat → choose a toy → sit and enjoy the evening → look at the moon → head off) is clear and the images support it.
- Several lines feel like phonics padding rather than natural speech (e.g., “I stay to see.” “Tap, tap, tap!” hanging alone; repeated “so X”).
- A few mismatches or oddities: “toy bird with soft hair” (birds have feathers), “stool” mentioned though the picture could be read as a bench in a rug stall, and “fair” might not match a souq setting.
- In places the text relies on the picture to make sense; the words should carry the story by themselves.

### 3. Level appropriateness
- Current manuscript often has 3 sentences per page (spec asks for 2).
- Many words breach L2 structure (initial clusters; untaught graphemes), so it’s not phonically secure for this level.
- It does use a good spread of L1/L2 sounds, but several lines are blocked by decodability issues.

### 4. Proposed rewrite
Two sentences per page, 4–7 words each. All words use the cumulative graphemes or tricky words. Wording matches the images and keeps natural English.

Page 1
- We go to the shops at night.
- The lights are up high.

Page 2
- The lamps are so cool.
- I feel such joy.

Page 3
- A man taps at night.
- I sit and see him.

Page 4
- Mum gets hot food for us.
- I sip the hot soup.

Page 5
- I see a toy bird.
- Mum said, “Yes, you can.”

Page 6
- I sit and hug it.
- The air is cool now.

Page 7
- I see the big moon.
- It is low and round.

Page 8
- My night at the shops is good.
- I say good night, big moon.

Total words: 86 (within 80–130 target).

Focus-sound coverage in the rewrite: ay (say), ee (see, feel), igh (night, high), ow (now, low), oo (cool, moon, good, food), ar (— minimal), or (for), air (air), ir (bird), ou (soup, round), oy (toy).

### 5. Notes for the human editor
- I replaced “souq” with “shops” because q without u is outside the taught graphemes; “market” would add forbidden final clusters. If you must keep “souq”, it would need to be pre‑taught as a topic word, which the programme rules currently prohibit.
- I avoided “drum”, “stool”, “stars”, “sky”, etc., because of initial cluster rules. If your L2.6 policy allows a small set of initial clusters, we could reinstate those words.
- I used the tricky word “said” to give natural speech without “says”.
- Coverage: every new long‑vowel/digraph set appears except a clean “ar” example. If you want explicit “ar”, the only natural options I can see are “car” (if a car can be sketched in background) or swapping “food” for “tart”/“jar”, but those bring final clusters. Current version keeps the language natural and secure.
