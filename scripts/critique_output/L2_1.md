### 1. Phonics audit
Page 1
- “can’t” – not decodable. Contraction not taught and the vowel sound /ar/ in can’t is irregular here. Replace with “can not”.

All other words on pages 1–8 are decodable with the cumulative graphemes or are on the tricky‑word list. “lights” is acceptable as -s on a decodable base word; do not treat the final -ts as a blend issue.

### 2. Narrative critique
- The story is clear: the child loses a toy cat at dusk, goes out with Dad to look, notices lights and the moon, feels sad, is comforted, then spots and hugs the toy. The emotional arc works.
- Text-image match is mostly good. Page 6 shows Dad comforting under a bright light; “He hugs me in the light” fits.
- Language is generally natural and not list-like. One line feels slightly programmatic: “I can see in the shop,” but it still matches the art.
- Repetition of “look/see/light” supports decoding without feeling like padding.

### 3. Level appropriateness
- Sentences mostly meet the 4–7 words requirement, but two do not: “The day ends.” (3 words) and “It is night.” (3 words).
- Current word count is c. 80; fine for the target range.
- Good cumulative practice across L1/L2 code, with clear use of focus sounds ay, ee, igh alongside earlier code (sh, oo, ow, oy).

### 4. Proposed rewrite
Two sentences per page, 4–7 words each. All words use allowed graphemes or tricky words. Text keeps the same storyline and image alignment.

Page 1
- The day ends now.
- I can not see my toy cat.

Page 2
- We go out to look.
- It is night now.

Page 3
- Look at the lights.
- I can see in the shop.

Page 4
- It is dim on the way.
- I need a light.

Page 5
- Look, a light up high!
- It is the moon.

Page 6
- Dad can see I am sad.
- He hugs me in the light.

Page 7
- I see my toy cat!
- Dad and I run to it.

Page 8
- I hug my toy cat.
- I say, ‘Yay, my toy!’

Approx. word count: 83.

### 5. Notes for the human editor
- “can not” is chosen to avoid the non-decodable “can’t”. It reads naturally enough in context.
- I kept “I can see in the shop” to align with the picture of the lit shop window and to practise ee/igh without forcing odd phrasing.
- Check art makes it clear this is a toy cat (size and stitched look) so no confusion with a real cat.
- -s inflections used: lights, hugs – both permitted by programme rules.
