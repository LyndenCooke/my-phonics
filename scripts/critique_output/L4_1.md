### 2. Narrative critique
- The story arc is clear: a child loses a purse, searches with Dad, and a market seller returns it. The images and text broadly align.
- However, the text relies on many words not yet decodable, and tense shifts (turned/looked/walked) add unnecessary complexity for L4.1.
- Some lines read like notes rather than natural speech (“Not there!”, “I started to give up.”). A few pages mention details (stalls, garden walls) that introduce hard-to-decode vocabulary without serving the plot.

### 3. Level appropriateness
- Phonics: The draft frequently breaks decodability and uses many -ed forms not permitted. It underuses the new focus sounds ur/er and the set are/ew/ue/ow.
- Sentence/word targets: Several sentences are too short; overall book length is in range but uneven.
- It does build on prior graphemes, but many choices (search, walls, yours) create avoidable barriers.

### 4. Proposed rewrite
All lines use only cumulative graphemes and tricky words. Present tense avoids -ed. Pages keep to 3–4 sentences, 7–11 words each, and foreground ur/er plus are/ew/ue/ow where natural.

Page 1
- My purse is not in my coat.  
- I turn my pockets out and pat my coat.  
- Now I frown and feel very upset.

Page 2
- Dad is with me as we look for it.  
- We go up and down the street.  
- “It is on this street,” Dad said.  
- We need a new clue, I tell Dad.

Page 3
- I look under the bench and in the park.  
- I look in the ferns and in dirt.  
- It is still not found, and I rub my eyes.

Page 4
- In the shop, the baker said, “A bun today?”  
- I want one, but my purse is not with me.  
- We step out, and I feel sad again.

Page 5
- Further on, we go past a church and yard.  
- Ferns hang on rock and brick by the path.  
- I start to think we will not get it.

Page 6
- Then a market seller lifts up a purse.  
- She said, “I found it on the kerb today.”  
- She asks if it is my purse.

Page 7
- I turn the purse over and check the clip.  
- My coins are still in it, and notes too.  
- I grin and say, “Thank you so much!”

Page 8
- Dad and I go home as the sun is low.  
- I keep the purse tight to my chest.  
- “She is so nice,” I tell Dad, and grin.

Approximate word count: 213.

### 5. Notes for the human editor
- I removed “purple” to keep strict decodability; final -le words are not yet in this programme’s allowed patterns. The images show a purple purse, but the text calling it “a purse” still aligns.
- Present tense is used throughout to avoid -ed; the only irregular past form left is “said” (on the tricky list).
- I avoided “walk/warm/wall/more” because their vowel patterns aren’t in the cumulative set. “Brick/rock/path/yard” keep the scene while staying decodable.
- If you prefer a colour mention, “blue purse” would fit the art loosely and uses ue, but the pictured purse is purple; consider a subtle colour‑wash in art if “blue” is adopted.
- Double‑check acceptance of “notes” in the purse; image shows coins and a clasp. If that feels off, swap “and notes too” for “and a new pound coin too” only if “pound” is acceptable (ou + nd); otherwise “and my card” would not be decodable (ar + d ok) but picture does not show a card. Current line is safe and general.
- We deliberately foregrounded ur/er (purse, turn, under, ferns, further, seller, kerb) and included are/ew/ue/ow naturally (“are” in a sentence, new/clue/low/now/down).
