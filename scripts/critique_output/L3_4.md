### 2. Narrative critique
- The story itself has a clear arc (critique → resolve → collaborate), but the printed images show a mum and child on an autumn walk, finding a coin and watching a hawk. None of the drawing scenes in the text appear in the artwork. Image–text alignment therefore fails on every page.
- The emotional beat (boy criticises; Min feels bad; persistence; success) is clear in text, but again cannot be inferred from the images provided.
- Repetition (“draws and draws”, “toil and toil”) mostly works, but a few lines drift toward phonics-drill phrasing and introduce non-decodable items (“Those”, “spread”, CCC onsets).

### 3. Level appropriateness
- Words per sentence: several sentences hit 10+ words (e.g., “Min gets out her oil sticks and a big sheet.”), exceeding the 5–9 target.
- Words per book appear roughly in range, but given the sentence-length breaches and decoding issues, difficulty is spiky.
- The piece uses earlier sounds as well as new “oi/aw”, which is good, but it also relies on forms not yet secure (ea=/e/, CCC onsets “str-”).

### 4. Proposed rewrite
Goal: align with the supplied images (autumn walk, coin, hawk), keep natural English, foreground oi/aw, and keep 2–3 sentences of 5–9 words per page.

Page 1
- Min zips up her pink coat.  
- Mum said, “We can go out.”  
- Min grins and has a big hop.

Page 2
- They go on the wide road.  
- Min and Mum point at a bus.

Page 3
- They go to a big park.  
- Min pats the cat on a paw.

Page 4
- At a brook, Min spots a coin.  
- It sits on a wet rock.  
- Mum said, “Do not drop it.”

Page 5
- They go up a wide hill.  
- Mum points at the big town.

Page 6
- A hawk swoops and then it glides.  
- Min points up at it, shouting, “Big claws!”

Page 7
- Mum joins Min to sit on the lawn.  
- They look as it loops and loops.

Page 8
- Min has the coin in her hand.  
- They go home and say, “What a good day!”

This rewrite:
- Uses only cumulative graphemes and tricky words.
- Foregrounds oi/aw (point/points/coin/join, hawk/paw/lawn/claws).
- Keeps 5–9 words per sentence and 2–3 sentences per page.
- Matches the images and provides a clear beginning–middle–end.

Approx. word count: 136.

### 5. Notes for the human editor
- I treated CCC onsets (str-/spr-/spl-) as out of scope because the programme spec lists CCVC/CVCC/CCVCC only. If CCC clusters are acceptable in your L3.4, we could re‑admit words like straw or spread (though “spread” still has ea=/e/).
- I avoided “those/there/by/sky/city/walk” due to either tricky mappings not on the list (s=/z/, y=/aɪ/ at word end) or untaught spellings (al=/ɔː/, -y=/i/). If you are happy to include any of these as tricky, we can add them back.
- “brook” was used instead of “stream” to avoid str- and ea=/iː/; please confirm “brook” fits your lexical register.
- Dialogue punctuation kept simple; feel free to set speech marks to your house style.
