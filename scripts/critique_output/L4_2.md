### 2. Narrative critique
- The storyline is clear: child hears a sound, goes out with Mum, spots a brown owl and owlets, watches them being fed, and heads home. The emotional arc (from worry to wonder to care) comes through.
- Image–text match is strong across pages (window at dusk, woods, owl on branch, hole with owlets, walk home).
- However, many sentences are too short for L4.2, with several one–three word lines (“Wow!”, “Look!”). The brief lines pad the page count without building fluency.
- Language is mostly natural, but reliance on -ed past tense conflicts with decoding constraints for this level.

### 3. Level appropriateness
- Sentence length: Many original sentences fall below the 7–11 words target.
- Words/book: Likely within or just above target, but sentence-length inconsistency makes fluency practice uneven.
- Phonics coverage: Good range, including new sounds are/er/ow; less use of ur, ew/ue. Several non‑allowed patterns (-ed, soft c) creep in.
- Word structure: Mostly appropriate; includes some multi‑syllable words (together, window, under). Consonant clusters are fine for this level.

### 4. Proposed rewrite
Notes:
- Present tense used to avoid -ed endings.
- Every sentence 7–11 words. Three or four sentences per page.
- New focus sounds foregrounded: are, er/ur, ow, ew/ue.

Page 1
- The sky turns deep blue outside the wide window.
- From deep in the trees comes a loud howl.
- I stare out, but all I see are shadows.

Page 2
- I say, “Can we go and look, Mum?”
- She gets my thick coat and warm boots.
- We set off down the dark path together.
- A cool wind blows on my bare cheeks.

Page 3
- Then Mum stops and stares up at the trees.
- Mum says, “Look up there!” and points with a grin.
- A big brown owl sits on a bare branch.

Page 4
- I stare at the owl, and the owl stares back.
- Mum says, “How rare! You do not see this.”
- I want to get close, but I do not dare.

Page 5
- The owl spreads its wings and swoops down close to me.
- I gasp, “Wow,” as it lands on a stump.
- The owl turns its head to look right at me.

Page 6
- The owl looks up at a hole high in the tree.
- Out come soft cheeps from two small owlets.
- Mum says, “She must look after them, so we keep back.”

Page 7
- The owl swoops back with a mouse in its claws.
- The owlets crowd round and cheep as Mum and I grin.
- What a rare sight to share on a dark night.

Page 8
- We head home on the path under the new moon.
- I say, “I want to care for them again.”
- Mum takes my hand and says, “We will, one day.”

Approximate word count: 230–240.

### 5. Notes for the human editor
- I shifted to present tense to avoid the non‑permitted -ed past-tense forms. If your scope for L4.2 does include -ed, we could revert to past and simplify a few lines.
- I removed “faces” to avoid soft c; replaced with “owlets” and descriptions that stay fully decodable.
- Coverage of the focus graphemes: are (stare, rare, care), er (under, after), ur (turns), ow (howl, owl, down, crowd, round), ew (new), ue (blue). If you want even more “ur”, we could swap “cool wind” for “a sharp, cool burst of wind.”
- “mouse” is decodable with ou + se; if you prefer to avoid final “se” = /s/, change to “a fat bug” or “a moth,” though those may not match the art.
- Dialogue punctuation kept simple; no semicolons. All sentences meet the 7–11 words guideline and should read as natural spoken English.
