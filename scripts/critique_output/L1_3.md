### 2. Narrative critique
- The text tells a simple, clear story: child brings home a fish in a bag, worries it is cramped, tries a cup, then gets a tank and everyone is content. The images match this sequence.
- Emotional arc is clear (sad fish → solution → content fish/child).
- Repetition (“fish”, “bag”, “tank”) supports decoding well.
- Weaknesses:
  - “Wish, wish!” feels like padding and not natural spoken English.
  - Two sentences per page exceed the L1.3 spec of one sentence per page.
  - Non‑decodable words “have” and “happy” break the programme rules.

### 3. Level appropriateness
- Phonics coverage: good use of earlier sounds plus focus graphemes sh (fish) and nk (tank). Word structures are L1‑appropriate.
- However, the current layout has 2 sentences per page. If restricted to 1 sentence of 3–5 words per page (6 pages), the book will sit around 18–30 words total, which cannot meet the stated 40–80 words/book target. This is a spec conflict to resolve at programme level.
- Aside from that, vocabulary and syntax are age‑appropriate and mostly natural.

### 4. Proposed rewrite
One sentence per page, 3–5 words, all decodable/tricky, aligned to the images.

Page 1: I get a fish.
Page 2: The fish is sad.
Page 3: The cup is not big.
Page 4: I get a tank.
Page 5: The tank has a fish.
Page 6: I am with the fish.

### 5. Notes for the human editor
- Spec conflict: With 6 pages and 1 sentence (3–5 words) per page, the maximum is ~30 words. To reach 40–80 words you’ll need either more pages or permission for two sentences on some pages.
- I avoided “happy” due to final y=/ee/ not being taught. “glad” would be natural but uses an initial cluster (gl), which is not allowed before L3. Hence the neutral “I am with the fish.”
- If you prefer a more explicit positive ending while staying fully decodable, consider: “I am not sad.” (also 4 words) or “I am so glad” only if “so” is later added as a tricky word and initial clusters policy is relaxed for “glad” (currently not allowed).
- I dropped the interjection “Wish, wish!” as it reads like phonics padding and doesn’t reflect natural speech.
