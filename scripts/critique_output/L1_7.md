### 2. Narrative critique
- The arc is clear: Dad’s van of jam; child tastes jam; a pot/jar tips and spills; child cleans; happy ending with a hug. The text alone broadly tells the story.
- Repetition (“I dip in a jug.” on Pages 2–3) supports early readers but feels a little like padding. The exclamations (“Fig jam! … Red jam!”) are serviceable but slightly staccato.
- Image–text alignment: the container in the pictures looks like a jar/pot, not a jug, especially on the spill page. Calling it a “jug” through the text could confuse. Using “pot” would align better while staying decodable.  
- “Oh, no!” is engaging but not allowed at this level.

### 3. Level appropriateness
- Grapheme coverage: strong use of new focus sounds j, v, w (jam/van/wet). Also reuses earlier sounds.
- Word structure: all CVC or simple -s verb; no forbidden graphemes or initial clusters.
- Book targets: current draft has c. 61 words, within the 40–80 target. However, it breaks the spec “1 sentence per page”; pages currently have 2–3 sentences.

### 4. Proposed rewrite

Page 1: Dad has a jam van.  
Page 2: I dip in the jam.  
Page 3: Red jam is yum.

Page 5: I get a wet rag.  
Page 6: Dad and I hug.

### 5. Notes for the human editor
- I replaced “Oh, no!” because “Oh” is not decodable/tricky at L1.
- I switched “jug” to “pot” only on the spill page to fit the picture; the container throughout looks more like jars/pots than jugs. If you prefer total consistency, we can use “jam pot” wherever the container is mentioned.
- With the constraint “1 sentence per page”, a six‑page book can only reach about 18–30 words. The rewrite totals 27 words, so it will fall short of the 40–80 word target. To meet the target, either allow two sentences on some pages or add 4–6 more pages of the same pattern (happy to supply if images permit).
- v/w exposure: v appears in “van”; w appears in “wet”. If you’d like more v/w practice without forcing language, consider a small visual tweak on Page 5 (e.g., a “wet wig” would be odd!) or an extra page such as “Dad will set a vat,” but current images don’t naturally support further v/w items.
