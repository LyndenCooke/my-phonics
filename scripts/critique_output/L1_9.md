### 2. Narrative critique
- The storyline is clear: Nan chops, fries chips, serves them, and the child eats with a dip. The images and text generally align.
- Voice mostly sounds natural, but several pages use fragments (“Chips in a hot pan.” “Chips with Nan!”) and heavy chant-like repetition (“chops, chops, chops”, “yum, yum, yum”, “munch, munch, munch”). Some repetition is fine, but it starts to feel like padding rather than narrative.
- “I got …” (pp.3–4) sounds a bit off in British English here; “I get …” would read more naturally in present-tense narration.
- Programme spec says one sentence per page. The current draft has two per page, so it doesn’t meet that requirement.

### 3. Level appropriateness
- Grapheme coverage is appropriate and foregrounds the new sounds ch and th while re-using earlier code (sh, ck, short vowels). Word structures stay within CVC/digraph limits.
- One violation: “munch” (nch final cluster).
- Words/sentence length is fine, but the book has two sentences per page. With that, total word count ≈47 (within 40–80). If we enforce “1 sentence per page”, a 6‑page book cannot hit 40–80 words; the maximum would be 30. See editor notes.

### 4. Proposed rewrite
Goal: one sentence per page (3–5 words), fully decodable, natural English, matching the images.

Page 1
- I hum as Nan chops.

Page 2
- Nan chops thin and thick.

Page 3
- Chips go in a pan.

Page 4
- I get a chip.

Page 5
- I dip a chip in.

Page 6
- Chips with Nan!

Total words: 27.

### 5. Notes for the human editor
- The only phonics breach was “munch”. It’s replaced. If you want an onomatopoeic ending, “yum” is decodable but would make a second sentence; we could instead do “Chips with Nan, yum!” (still one sentence) if you’re happy with that tone.
- Because the spec fixes “1 sentence per page” and there are only six pages, the rewritten book cannot reach the 40–80 word target. To meet the count without breaking level rules, consider adding 2–4 pages, e.g.:
  - “Nan tips chips in.” (serving) 
  - “I sit with Nan.” (scene beat; image tweak needed as child stands now)
  - “That chip is thin.” (contrast with thick)
  - “I sip a dip.” (if a small sip/lick image works; uses only taught code)
- Page 3 image shows both a hot pan and the child with a plate. The rewrite focuses on the pan action; it still aligns, but if you want the plate referenced explicitly, swap to “I get a big dish.” (all decodable) and let the pan be implied by the steam in the art.
- “I got …” changed to “I get …” for more natural present-tense narration consistent with the rest of the book.
- Double-check that the utensil looks like a pan (not a wok/fryer) to keep “pan” accurate. If it reads as a “pot”, “pan” should still be acceptable visually.
