### 2. Narrative critique
- The storyline is clear: child plays with a toy car, loses it, calls Mum, they search, and find it. The images and text generally match.
- However, tense drifts between past and present and relies heavily on -ed forms that aren’t decodable for this level.
- Repetition of “around and around” helps fluency, but “around” itself isn’t decodable here, so the repetition becomes padding and creates decoding barriers.
- A few word choices don’t fit the level (“path”), and several pages exceed the “2 sentences per page” rule.

### 3. Level appropriateness
- Current text often exceeds two sentences per page and includes many non‑decodable -ed verbs.
- It does use earlier sounds, and it includes the focus sounds ou/oy, but phonics control needs tightening.
- After rewrite (below): 2 sentences per page, each 4–7 words; total 86 words (within 80–130). Frequent, natural use of ou/oy alongside earlier code.

### 4. Proposed rewrite
Page 1
- I go out with my toy car.  
- I zoom it round and round.

Page 2
- I zoom it far, far.  
- It gets loud, loud!

Page 3
- But it runs too far.  
- I can not see my toy.

Page 4
- I shout out, “Mum!”  
- I can not see it!

Page 5
- Mum runs out to me.  
- “We will look,” she said.

Page 6
- We look round the big rock.  
- We look round the shed.

Page 7
- Mum said, “Look, I found it!”  
- I jump and shout, “Joy!”

Page 8
- I hug my toy and Mum.  
- I say, “Thank you, Mum!”

All words use only the cumulative graphemes or tricky words for this level.

### 5. Notes for the human editor
- I switched to present‑tense forms to avoid -ed endings, which aren’t yet secure at L2.5.
- I replaced “around” with the British preposition/adverb “round,” which is fully decodable and natural.
- “Path” was removed due to the non‑phonetic long a in many UK accents; we kept the action emphasis instead.
- Dialogue punctuation is kept simple. If you prefer fewer quotation marks, we can convert to reported speech without changing decodability.
- Focus sounds ou/oy appear often (out, round, loud, shout, found, joy, toy) while mixing in earlier code for natural language.
