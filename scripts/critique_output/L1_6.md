### 2. Narrative critique
- The story is clear: a fox keeps climbing and falling; the child solves it with a soft mat and ends with a hug. The repetition of “fell off” gives predictable structure and supports decoding.
- Image–text match is mostly good, but “wall” is not decodable and forces an off‑scope word on Pages 3. Also the mat pictured looks like a cushion; calling it a “mat” avoids an untaught word.
- Some lines are padding (“Oh, Fox!”) and include untaught graphemes. The final page currently has three sentences; one strong, decodable sentence would read more naturally at this level.

### 3. Level appropriateness
- Grapheme coverage: good emphasis on f, l, ff, ll via fox, fell, off, hill.
- Word/sentence: Several pages have two or three sentences; programme spec says 1 sentence per page, 3–5 words.
- Words/book: The original hits 40–80 words, but after enforcing “1 sentence per page, 3–5 words,” a 6‑page book will top out around 30 words. That target is unattainable with only six pages unless the pagination increases.

### 4. Proposed rewrite
(One sentence per page. 3–5 words. All decodable/tricky OK. Aligned to the images as far as the code allows.)

- Page 1: Fox is on a log.
- Page 2: Fox fell off the log.
- Page 3: Fox fell off a rock.
- Page 4: Fox fell off the hill.
- Page 5: Fox, sit on the mat.
- Page 6: I hug Fox.

Total words: 28.

### 5. Notes for the human editor
- “Wall” is off‑code. I’ve used “rock” on Page 3 to stay decodable and still plausible with the stone wall art. If possible, adjust the illustration to a big rock/boulder or a more “rocky” look to tighten alignment.
- The cushion in the art reads as a “cushion” or “pad,” but “cushion” is off‑code (‑ion). I’ve kept “mat”; consider tweaking the artwork to look more mat‑like.
- The words/book target (40–80) conflicts with “1 sentence per page, 3–5 words” on a 6‑page book. To meet the word target without breaking decodability or naturalness, we’d need 8–12 pages, or relax the 1‑sentence rule.
- Removed “have,” “oh,” and “fall” for code reasons. Kept plenty of f/ff/l/ll practice with natural phrasing.
