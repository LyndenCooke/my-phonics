### 2. Narrative critique
- The story arc is clear: Kai waits in the rain for a boat, spots it, and reunites with Dad. Emotion builds from boredom/annoyance to hope and joy.
- Image–text alignment has major mismatches:
  - Page 1 text says Kai “stands at the coast”, but the image shows him sitting on a step.
  - Page 2 text mentions pacing and a snail; image shows running with a coat. Wording “paces the road” sounds unnatural.
  - Page 3 image shows the snail, but text jumps to “a shape floats on the foam”.
  - Page 4 text is about the boat and its stripe; image shows Kai in a muddy puddle with a muddy coat.
  - Page 5 image finally shows the striped sail; text focuses mainly on fish and uses “front”.
  - Pages 6–8 broadly align.
- Some dialogue feels padded or adult (“Even the snail wins the race!”) and several very short sentences break reading rhythm.

### 3. Level appropriateness
- Several sentences are under the 5–9 word target and a few contain out‑of‑scope code (“paces”, “even”, “race”, “front”, “voice”, “worth”).
- Word count overall is fine, but phonics accuracy is inconsistent.
- The text uses earlier code as well as ai/oa, but some lines feel like drills rather than natural speech.

### 4. Proposed rewrite
Goal: keep the same plot, foreground ai/oa naturally, meet 2–3 sentences per page with 5–9 words each, and match the images.

Page 1
- Kai sits on a step in rain.  
- He waits for a boat to sail.  
- The sea is still and flat.

Page 2
- He groans, then runs on in rain.  
- It is so slow, he said.  
- His coat hangs from one hand.

Page 3
- He stops and looks at a snail.  
- It is so slow, he moans.  
- Rain drips on his nose and chin.

Page 4

- Wet mud slaps his legs and feet.  
- His coat is a mess on the road.

Page 5
- Then he spots a shape on the sea.  
- A boat with a red stripe sails.

Page 6

- Kai shouts back as loud as he can.  
- “Dad! Is that you?” he yells.

Page 7
- The boat docks and Dad runs to Kai.  
- Dad scoops him up in a hug.

Page 8

### 5. Notes for the human editor
- I replaced all soft‑c words (paces, race, voice) and the irregular front, worth. If soft c is actually in scope for your L3.5, you could reinstate one instance, but it’s safer to avoid here.
- I removed the possessive “Dad’s”. Some programmes allow it; others defer apostrophes until later.
- I aligned text to the images: snail appears on p3, muddy puddle/coat on p4, the striped sail on p5, Dad’s call on p6, hug on p7, crate/snail happy ending on p8.
- I kept the focus sounds ai and oa prominent (rain, wait, sail, coat, road, boat, rail, snail) while mixing in earlier code for natural language.
- If the art on p1 must show eating, we could tweak to “Kai sits on a step with a roll,” but “roll” uses the ‘o’ as /əʊ/ and double‑l; still decodable, but only add if the prop is editorially important.
