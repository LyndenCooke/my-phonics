### 2. Narrative critique
- The story arc is clear: child gets a dog; dog jumps in mud; chaos; attempted clean‑up fails; Mum brings a tub and the dog is cleaned. Text largely matches the images.
- Some lines sound stilted or unnatural for everyday speech, notably “I mop the dog.”
- Repetition “Mud, mud, mud!” works for rhythm but feels like padding.
- Page 5 has a decodability breach (“me”).
- Multiple sentences per page break the L1.2 brief of one short sentence per page.

### 3. Level appropriateness
- Current manuscript exceeds the “1 sentence per page” rule; several pages have two or three sentences.
- With many short sentences, the total word count is roughly in target (c. 59), but if we enforce one 3–5‑word sentence per page across six pages, the book will fall under 40 words. You may need extra pages to meet the programme word‑count target.
- Phonics coverage is appropriate and uses prior sounds; it also foregrounds new focus sounds m, d, g, o in common words (mud, dog, got, Mum).

### 4. Proposed rewrite
One sentence per page, 3–5 words, fully decodable/tricky‑word compliant, natural English, aligned to images.

- Page 1: I got a dog.
- Page 2: The dog ran in mud.
- Page 3: The dog is a mess.
- Page 4: I rub the dog.
- Page 5: I am a mess!
- Page 6: No mud, no mess!

Total words: 26.

### 5. Notes for the human editor
- Word count: With six pages and one short sentence per page, we cannot reach 40–80 words. Options: add 2–4 pages, or allow one additional short sentence on selected spreads (e.g., Page 6: “Mum got a tub. The dog sat in it.”).
- “I rub the dog” replaces “I mop the dog” for more natural English while still fitting the art (the child is scrubbing with a brush/sponge).
- If you prefer to reference Mum explicitly on the last spread, an alternative single sentence is “Mum got a tub.” (4 words), but it gives a weaker sense of resolution than “No mud, no mess!”
- Avoid “me” at this level; if you want the child to say they’re muddy, “I am a mess!” or “I am in mud!” are both compliant and natural.
