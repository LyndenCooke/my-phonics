### 2. Narrative critique
- The story arc is clear: a cautious child resists Gran’s food, tastes it, loves it, then reassures his sister. The repetition of “looks suspicious” supports the emotional journey and humour.
- Images and text broadly align (lemons, bread/tomato, pot of pasta, frozen lemon dessert, sister entering). However, specific Italian terms and place names used in the text (Amalfi, bruschetta, limoncello, granita) are not supported phonically and are unnecessary for understanding; they also risk misalignment if we must replace them with decodable descriptions (“tomato toast”, “lemon ice”).
- The language quality is strong and natural, but some long, descriptive sentences push beyond level expectations and introduce many off‑scope spellings.

### 3. Level appropriateness
- Focus sounds are well foregrounded (suspicious, cautious, gracious, luscious, precious, nutritious, scrumptious, delicious, ambitious). Good.
- Many sentences exceed the 10–15 word target; the book is likely over 500 words.
- Too many off‑scope spellings and foreign terms break the “fully decodable” rule. 
- It does build on earlier graphemes, but several plurals with -es/-ves appear, which the programme forbids.

### 4. Proposed rewrite
Notes
- Replace non‑decodable names/terms with decodable equivalents while keeping the Amalfi‑like setting via pictures.
- Use only allowed graphemes/tricky words.
- 5 sentences per page; 10–15 words each; ~440 words total.
- Foreground cious/tious/ous throughout.

Page 1
- Luke stood in Gran's spacious kitchen with his arms crossed tight.  
- Sun streamed through the open window onto bright clifftop homes and sea.  
- Gran set a glass dish on the bench beside the sink.  
- Inside sat one thing green, one thing red, and one Luke did not trust.  
- He frowned and asked, "What is that, Gran? It looks suspicious to me."

Page 2
- Gran smiled, gracious and patient, as if she knew this cautious boy.  
- She picked a ripe lemon from the tree beside the door.  
- Its skin looked glossy and luscious, like sunshine trapped in a ball.  
- She sliced it open, and a sharp, fresh smell burst into kitchen.  
- Luke sniffed and said, "That smells not bad," though he stayed cautious.

Page 3
- Gran drizzled green oil over sliced tomato with a pinch of salt.  
- Then she grated lemon rind on top and mixed it well.  
- She set a plate of crusty bread beside the dish, and Luke peered at it.  
- She said, "This is tomato toast. It is simple and nutritious."  
- But the smell was delicious, with bright lemon, good oil, and warm crust.

Page 4
- Next, Gran filled a big pot with hot water and set it on the hob.  
- She tipped in long pasta and stirred it with a wooden spoon.  
- She smiled and said, "Lemon pasta, with cream, a squeeze of lemon, and pepper."  
- It smelled absolutely delicious, and Luke stepped closer, though still a little cautious.  
- He said, "I am not eating that," but his feet did not move.

Page 5
- Gran set one last dish on the table, a small glass of lemon ice.  
- She said, "Frozen, sweet, and scrumptious. A precious treat on this sunny coast."  
- Luke looked at the tomato toast, the pasta, and the lemon ice.  
- All three smelled delicious, and he had to admit he was hungry.  
- He said, "I may be cautious, but my tummy is very ready."

Page 6
- He reached out slowly and picked up one small piece of toast.  
- He took a tiny bite, and his eyes went wide at once.  
- It was delicious, bright tomato and lemon across his mouth, so he ate more.  
- Next, he tried the pasta in his bowl, still hot and glossy.  
- He said, "Gran, this is the best food I have ever tasted."

Page 7
- The front door clicked open, and small feet pattered down the hall.  
- Luke's sister Sophie ran in, her yellow headband bright against her dark curls.  
- She stopped at the table and gave the lemon ice a suspicious look.  
- "What is that?" she asked, pointing at the frosty glass on the table.  
- She said, "It looks suspicious!" Luke grinned, remembering he had said that too.

Page 8
- Luke lifted the lemon ice and held it out for his sister.  
- He said, "Trust me. I was suspicious too, but Gran's food is nutritious."  
- He added, "And this is a precious, delicious treat on the whole coast."  
- Sophie took a cautious lick, and her face lit up at once.  
- Gran watched by the window and thought, "Feeding people you love is ambitious and delicious."

### 5. Notes for the human editor
- Names: I changed Luca → Luke and Sofia → Sophie to ensure full decodability. If you must retain Italian names, we will breach the programme rule; please confirm preference.
- Italian terms: bruschetta/limoncello/granita/Amalfi are replaced with decodable paraphrases (tomato toast, lemon ice, sunny coast). Images still make sense with these generic labels.
- “Most” was swapped for “best” throughout because “most” isn’t decodable from the taught graphemes here.
- I avoided -es/-ves plurals (tomatoes/leaves/houses). If the scope later permits -es, we can restore natural plurals.
- I removed em dashes and colons per punctuation guidance and kept sentences within 10–15 words.
- If you want more overt coverage of -able/-ible, we can add “suitable” or “edible” in one or two spots without harming flow.
