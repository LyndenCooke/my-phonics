### 2. Narrative critique
- The storyline is clear: visit a night market, child gets separated, finds a landmark, reunites, then eats together. The text alone largely carries the plot and the images support it.
- Emotional arc is evident (wonder → panic → problem-solving → relief).
- Some phrasing is unnatural for spoken English (“Mum spoke”; repeated “We see…”; “But Mum is not there.”).
- Repetition works for pace (“I run past… I run past…”) but page 2’s “We see…” twice risks feeling list-like.
- Tense shifts are inconsistent (past on p1, then present/past mix later).

### 3. Level appropriateness
- Sentence length: several sentences are under the 5–9 word target (“And I did.”; “Then… I see Mum!”).
- Book length likely within target, but phonics breaches (elephants/noodle/stall/-ed) make it unsuitable as-is.
- Good recycling of earlier code plus the focus spellings o-e and u-e (“close, stone, cute, froze, huge, spoke/gave”). More natural phrasing can keep these while fixing decodability.

### 4. Proposed rewrite
(2–3 sentences per page, each 5–9 words. Only cumulative graphemes and tricky words used. Aligns with images.)

Page 1
- Mum and I go to a night market.
- It is huge and bright at night.
- “Stay close to me,” Mum said. So I keep close, all the time.

Page 2
- We see cute stone pets with long noses.
- We see bright pots of hot food.
- The food smelt so good!

Page 3
- Then a man runs into me!
- I spin round and round.
- I can not see Mum.

Page 4
- It is dark and loud now.
- I feel so lost and sad.
- “Mum!” I shout, but Mum is not with me.

Page 5
- I see the stone pets again.
- We went past those, I think.
- Mum must be close now!

Page 6
- I run past the bright lights.
- I run past the food stand.
- Then I see Mum at last!

Page 7
- Mum gave me a huge tight hug.
- “You are safe!” she said.
- I do not let go.

Page 8
- We sit close and eat hot pasta.
- “Stay close to me!” said Mum.
- I am so glad for home time!

Total words: about 150.

### 5. Notes for the human editor
- “Elephant(s)” replaced with “stone pets with long noses” to preserve image meaning without using ph. If you must keep “elephant”, it would need adding to the tricky list for this level (not recommended).
- “Stall” replaced with “stand” because ‘al’ for /or/ in stall/small isn’t in the taught code. If your scope allows ‘al’=/or/ at L3, you can restore “stall/small”.
- “Noodle(s)” swapped for “hot food”/“pasta” due to the -le ending. “Pasta” keeps the image sense of long strands; if that feels off-brand, consider “hot food in bowls”.
- Avoided -ed past forms (“bumped”, “scared”). Used present or irregular past instead.
- Kept and foregrounded o-e/u-e through close, stone, huge, gave, spoke; also used ea in “eat”.
- Double-check that “market” is acceptable at this stage (uses ar + CVC).  
- British usage: “smelt” retained. If you prefer “smelled”, it adds -ed and would breach level rules.
