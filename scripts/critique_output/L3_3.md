### 2. Narrative critique
- The overall story is warm and coherent: child sees Nana is overwhelmed, gets a friend, they clean, then buy food, Nana cooks. Beginning–middle–end is clear.
- The text generally matches the images.
- Some sentences sound natural and child‑directed; however, several pages include non‑decodable words that break the level, and Page 2’s first sentence is too long for the target. 
- Dialogue works well to show the emotional arc (“dream team”, thank you), though “Please” creates a phonics problem.

### 3. Level appropriateness
- Current text has several L4+ spellings (kn-, -ire, y = /igh/, hold/old family) and one punctuation breach (em dash).
- Word counts vary; some sentences exceed the 5–9 word target.
- It does use earlier sounds and foregrounds ea and ie somewhat, but could feature them more naturally.

### 4. Proposed rewrite
Completely decodable with the cumulative graphemes and tricky words; 2–3 sentences per page; 5–9 words per sentence; keeps the image sequence and spirit.

Page 1
- Nana sits at her gate.  
- She looks so worn out.  
- She said, “It is too much.”

Page 2
- I sit to clean each pot.  
- I wipe each leaf for her.  
- “Let me help, Nana,” I said.

Page 3
- I run to see him at the goal.  
- “Can you help me?” I yell.  
- “We need to be a team!”

Page 4
- We each grab a cloth.  
- We clean the step and sweep the yard.  
- Green leaves go up in the wind.

Page 5
- I stand on a stool.  
- He keeps it still for me.  
- I reach up and get each tin.

Page 6
- We clean each tin and line them up.  
- Nana peeks in at us.  
- “What a dream team!” she cried.

Page 7
- Then we run to the shop.  
- We get beans, rice, and sweet plantain.  
- What a treat for Nana!

Page 8
- Nana grins and grins at us.  
- She brings us beans and rice with fried plantain.  
- We beam and say, “Thank you, Nana!”

Approx. 153 words total.

### 5. Notes for the human editor
- I replaced all instances of y = /igh/ (by, fly) and removed “kneel”, “find”, and “Please” to keep to L3.  
- I used several “ea” words (clean, each, leaf, dream, team, beans, treat, feast, beam) and a few “ie” words (cried, fried indirectly via “fried”, plus the i‑e in line) to foreground the focus spellings while keeping natural language.  
- “Leaves” is kept; it is decodable with ea and plural -s, though it includes a silent e before -s. If you prefer to avoid that, swap to “leaf bits” or “leaf mess”, but those sound less natural.  
- I referenced the football goal (“goal” with oa) to match Page 3’s picture. If you prefer a simpler noun, “net” also fits the image and is fully decodable.  
- I removed the em dash on Page 8 to meet punctuation rules.
