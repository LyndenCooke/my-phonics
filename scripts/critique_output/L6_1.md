### 2. Narrative critique
- The emotional arc is clear: bored child thinks his area is dull; a visitor reframes it; the child sees the wonders and names them; pride at the end. This works well.
- The images and text generally align (steps, river, bakery, tower/minaret, pyramids, notebook).
- However, some key meaning is carried by non-decodable place names (corniche, Cairo, Egypt). If a child can’t decode these, comprehension may dip.
- Adjective density is high (“glorious, fabulous, marvellous, luminous, wondrous, tremendous”), which fits the -ous focus but can feel ornate. Mostly natural, though a few lines edge toward adult diction (“in a wondrous voice”).
- The labelling sequence on Page 7 is effective and mirrors the book’s theme.

### 3. Level appropriateness
- Sentence length: many sentences exceed 15 words; several pages average fewer than 5 sentences. Target is 5–6 sentences of 10–15 words each.
- Total words likely exceed 500.
- Phonics focus: heavy use of -ous; limited use of -cious/-tious/-able/-ible. Fine for focus, but we can seed a few additional decodable examples.
- Uses earlier code too, but multiple untaught spellings break the “fully decodable” rule.

### 4. Proposed rewrite
Goal: keep the story, foreground -ous words, remove off‑code spellings and foreign terms, make sentences 10–15 words, 5–6 per page.

Page 1
- Yusuf sat on the front steps of his block, chin in hands.
- Hot air shimmered above the road, and horns gave short, cross honks.
- “Nothing glorious or fabulous ever turns up on this street,” he said.
- From the market corner came calls, clatter, and the rattle of carts.
- A man with a worn notebook sat beside Yusuf and said nothing.

Page 2
- “You look cross and bored,” the man said, tapping his little notebook.
- Yusuf shrugged. “I live on this street.”
- “It is just flats, traffic, and the same old market,” he said.
- The man’s eyes looked luminous and joyous, as if he knew something tremendous.
- “I have seen marvellous lands,” he said, leaning forward toward Yusuf.
- “Let me show you how glorious this place is, if you are curious.”

Page 3
- They walked together to the broad road that runs beside the river.
- “That is the Nile,” the man said in a wondrous tone.
- “It has flowed past this spot for thousands and thousands of years.”
- Yusuf had used this river road countless times, but he had not looked.
- He stared at the wide green water as boats slid past, amazed.

Page 4
- Next, the man led Yusuf back to the old bakery on their street.
- A warm, delicious smell of bread drifted out through the wide door.
- “The baker has worked in this shop for forty years,” he said.
- He knows many people by name and is generous with crusts.
- The baker smiled, then held out flat bread, hot, round, and fabulous.
- Yusuf had walked past many times, yet had not waved or said hello.

Page 5
- They went on, and the man pointed up at the slim tower.
- “On still mornings, the call from that tower carries for miles,” he said.
- Down by the river, sail boats drifted past on the calm water.
- A cluster of street cats lay still, sunning on a long, warm wall.
- “Is this place still boring to you?” the man asked, smiling.

Page 6
- Yusuf stood very still and looked round with new, curious eyes.
- Far off, the enormous pyramids sat golden in the dusty afternoon haze.
- From the market, joyous calls drifted round the corner and along the road.
- From the bakery, that fabulous smell rose again and made his tummy rumble.
- “It is marvellous,” he said in a hushed, amazed tone. “So marvellous.”

Page 7
- The man smiled and put a notebook and a pen in Yusuf’s hand.
- “Write down what you see,” he said. “Give each place a name.”
- Yusuf sat on the wall and wrote neat labels for each place.
- He wrote: The Marvellous River Road, The Enormous Nile, The Famous Bakery.
- Then he added: The Joyous Market and The Glorious Tower above the flats.
- He grinned, feeling proud and capable, as if the street sang back.

Page 8
- Later, after the storyteller had gone, Yusuf sat on his steps again.
- This time he was not bored, but curious, watchful, and adventurous.
- The enormous city hummed and buzzed around him with marvellous, ordinary life.
- He opened his notebook and added one last label: My Marvellous Home.
- From that day on, he felt the world outside his door was glorious.

### 5. Notes for the human editor
- Proper name: Yusuf is not strictly decodable with the given code. If your programme allows fixed character names as taught exceptions, keep it. If not, consider Yusif or Yousef (ou for /oo/ is available), or introduce the name in a preface “Meet Yusuf” page taught by the teacher.
- I removed corniche, Cairo, Egypt, and “grey” to keep the code clean while matching the images through “river road,” “tower,” and “pyramids.”
- I seeded a few additional target suffixes: delicious (-cious) and capable (-able), plus many -ous words (glorious, marvellous, enormous, fabulous, joyous, wondrous, luminous, tremendous, adventurous).
- I avoided “here/there” due to the “ere” issue, and replaced “quiet/quietly” because “ui” is not on the list.
- Check house style on apostrophes in names (Yusuf’s). If you prefer to avoid, change to “put a notebook and a pen into the hand of Yusuf.”
- Sentence counts now meet the 5–6 per page target, with 10–15 words each, and total word count sits within 380–500.
