### 2. Narrative critique
- The core story is clear and engaging: child needs socks; looks in places; discovers the hen has red socks; gets them; celebrates.  
- Text generally matches images, except Page 5: the picture doesn’t show the hen pecking, but the text says “The hen pecks me!”.  
- Repetition of “I check the … / I get a sock. Not red!” gives a helpful pattern, but “Not red!” as a stand‑alone 2‑word sentence breaks the level spec and reads slightly like a drill line.  
- Emotional arc is simple and works: sad → search → discovery → success.

### 3. Level appropriateness
- Current manuscript breaches format rules: multiple sentences per page and some sentences have fewer than 3 words.  
- Several non‑decodable words appear (“have”, “me”, “so”, “happy”).  
- Phonics coverage is good for c/k/ck/e, but some pages feel written to hit sounds rather than natural speech.  
- With 6 pages, the original totals about 60 words (within the 40–80 target). However, the brief for this revision requires 1 sentence per page, 3–5 words; with 6 pages this necessarily reduces the book to roughly 18–30 words (below target). See editor note.

### 4. Proposed rewrite
One sentence per page, 3–5 words, fully decodable/tricky‑word compliant, and aligned to the images.

Page 1: No socks on.  
Page 2: A sock, not red.  
Page 3: A sock, not red.  
Page 4: The hen has red socks.  
Page 5: I get the socks.  
Page 6: Red socks on, I kick!

### 5. Notes for the human editor
- “Me” and “have/so/happy” were removed to stay within L1 decodability.  
- Page 5 artwork: ensure it shows the child taking the socks rather than being pecked, to match the new line.  
- The revised text foregrounds c/k/ck/e via sock/socks/kick/get/red/hen.  
- Because the layout is fixed at 6 pages and 1 sentence per page, the total word count is ~25, which is under the 40–80 target. If the programme needs the higher word count, consider expanding to 8–10 pages (e.g., add “bed” and “bag” search pages each as their own clear action lines) while keeping the same decodability constraints.  
- “A sock, not red.” is slightly elliptical but natural in child‑directed speech; if you prefer an alternative, “I get a sock.” also fits Page 2, with Page 3 then carrying the colour point: “Not red, a sock.” (still fully decodable).
