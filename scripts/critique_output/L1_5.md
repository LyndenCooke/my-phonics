### 2. Narrative critique
- The basic story is clear: a child plays chase with a pup; the pup hides in places (hut, bush), jumps into a tub, gets a rub, and ends with a hug.  
- Repetition of “Run, pup, run!” three times gives pace but starts to feel like padding; it takes space that could move the plot on.  
- Image–text alignment is mostly good (hut, bush, tub, rub, hug match the pictures).  
- Two problem words (“have”, “me”) break decodability.

### 3. Level appropriateness
- Graphemes are broadly appropriate and include the focus set u, r, h, b along with earlier sounds.  
- However, the manuscript uses two sentences per page; the programme spec for this level requires one sentence per page (3–5 words).  
- With 6 pages, sticking to 1 sentence per page would cap the book at 30 words, which is below the 40–80 word target. The current 49-word count is only achieved by having two sentences per page.

### 4. Proposed rewrite
One sentence per page, 3–5 words, fully decodable with the cumulative set, and matching each image.

Page 1: I run with the pup.  
Page 2: In the hut!  
Page 3: In the bush!  
Page 4: In the tub!  
Page 5: I rub the pup.  
Page 6: I hug the pup.

Total words: 22.

### 5. Notes for the human editor
- The biggest decodability issues were “have” and “me”. I removed both.  
- I used short, natural exclamations (“In the hut/bush/tub!”) so the place names stay explicit without breaking the 3–5 word limit. These are phrases a child might genuinely say while playing hide-and-seek with a pup.  
- Word-count target conflict: with 6 pages and a hard limit of 1 sentence of 3–5 words, the maximum possible is 30 words. To meet the 40–80 target you will need either:
  - more pages (e.g., 10–12), or
  - permission for occasional two-sentence pages at this level.  
- If the art team prefer the structure to name the building as a “shed”, an alternate Page 2 line is “In the shed!” (all decodable).  
- Check that “hut/shed” precisely matches the illustration choice before final sign‑off.
