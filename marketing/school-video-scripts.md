# MyPhonicsBooks for Schools — Video Scripts Pack

**Audience:** mixed school (phonics/English lead, SLT, classroom teacher)
**Access model positioned:** school plan (whole-class and whole-school, teacher dashboard, bulk assessment and book access)
**Use:** record these as screen-share walkthroughs and to-camera explainers for outreach, the website and LinkedIn.

Three scripts:

1. **Short explainer (60-90s)** — the hook. What it is, why it is different. To-camera or voiceover over b-roll.
2. **Full demo walkthrough (4-6 min)** — screen recording of the school platform, the main proposal video.
3. **Micro-clips (4 x ~30s)** — single-feature cuts for social and email.

Product facts used in every script (keep these accurate when you record):

- 118 decodable books across 8 levels: 73 Sound Books, 12 Blending Books, 33 Storybooks.
- 8-level progression aligned to Letters and Sounds (L1 to L8).
- Teaching pathway per level: Sound Book, then Blending Book, then Storybook.
- Every word in every book is decodable or a listed tricky word for that level.
- Adaptive assessment places each child at their working level; the dashboard shows who needs assessing and who is stuck.
- Built by a British primary teacher with QTS.
- Mapped to UK, Australian, US Common Core and IB PYP curricula.

## SCRIPT 1: Short Explainer (60-90s)

**Format:** to-camera, or voiceover over screen b-roll. Subtitles essential.
**Goal:** in under 90 seconds a phonics lead understands what it is and wants the demo.

### HOOK (0-10s)

**[On screen: you, to camera. Or a child reading a personalised book.]**

> "If you run phonics in a school, you already know the hard part is not teaching the sounds. It is knowing exactly where every child is, and giving each of them a book they can actually read."

### THE PROBLEM (10-25s)

**[On screen: a shelf of generic levelled readers; a teacher with a clipboard.]**

> "Most schemes sell books by age band, not by the sounds a child has been taught. So children meet words they have never been shown, they guess, and they start to believe they are bad at reading. And the teacher has no quick way to see who is falling behind."

### WHAT IT IS (25-55s)

**[On screen: screen capture. The school dashboard, then the library of three book types.]**

> "MyPhonicsBooks is a decodable reading programme and a teacher platform in one. A quick adaptive assessment places every child at their exact level, one to eight, mapped to Letters and Sounds. Then each child gets books where every single word is decodable at their level: a Sound Book to introduce the sounds, a Blending Book to practise, and a Storybook to read for real."

**[On screen: the level-distribution bar on the dashboard.]**

> "And you see the whole school on one screen: who is assessed, who is stuck, who is ready to move on. 118 books across eight levels, built by a British primary teacher with QTS."

### CTA (55-75s)

**[On screen: you, to camera. Lower third with website or email.]**

> "If your readers are guessing instead of decoding, this fixes the matching problem at the root. I will walk you through the full platform in a short demo. Link below to book it."

## SCRIPT 2: Full Demo Walkthrough (4-6 min)

**Format:** screen recording of the school platform, your voice over the top. Webcam bubble optional in a corner.
**Before you record:** seed the demo school so nothing reads zero. Sign in to the demo account. Close other tabs. Have the assessment and a sample book ready in pinned tabs so transitions are clean.
**Pacing:** calm and confident. Let each screen breathe for a second before you speak.

Two columns: what is on screen, and what you say.

### 0:00-0:30 — Open on the dashboard

| On screen | What you say |
|---|---|
| The school dashboard, populated: stats row, actions panel, level distribution bar. | "This is what a phonics lead sees the moment they log in. One school, every class, every reader, on a single screen. Before we click anything, look at the top: total pupils, how many are assessed, how many are not yet assessed, and how those children spread across the eight levels." |

### 0:30-1:15 — The "what to do next" panel

| On screen | What you say |
|---|---|
| Hover the amber actions panel. Point to each line. | "This is the part teachers tell me they wish they had. It tells you what needs doing, not just what exists. Children not yet assessed. Children sitting on the same level for four weeks or more, which usually means something has been missed. And classes with no assessment this month. No spreadsheets, no trawling through mark books. You log in and you know where to look." |
| Click into the level-distribution bar. | "And this bar is your whole-school phonics picture in one glance. Each colour is a level. If Year 1 is bunched at the bottom, you can see it and act on it this week." |

### 1:15-2:15 — The assessment

| On screen | What you say |
|---|---|
| Open the adaptive assessment. Run through two or three items. | "Placement is the foundation, so it has to be honest. The assessment is adaptive. It tests real sounds, real words and tricky words, and it adjusts as the child responds. It takes a few minutes per child and it gives you a working level, one to eight." |
| Show the result screen distinguishing passed from not tested. | "And it is honest about what it does not know. It separates what a child has passed from what was simply never tested. Most tools blur that line. This one does not, so you can trust the level it gives you and so can the parent." |

### 2:15-3:15 — The teaching pathway

| On screen | What you say |
|---|---|
| Open the Pathway page for a single level. Show Sound Book, then Blending Book, then Storybook. | "Once a child has a level, this is the route through it. Every level follows the same three steps. First the Sound Book, which introduces the new sounds in isolation. Then the Blending Book, where they practise pushing those sounds together into words. Then the Storybook, a real story where every word is decodable using only the sounds taught up to that point." |
| Pause on the Storybook. | "That last rule is the non-negotiable one. There are no surprise words. A child never has to guess. They decode, they succeed, and the confidence builds from there. It is the I-do, we-do, you-do sequence, built into the books." |

### 3:15-4:15 — The library

| On screen | What you say |
|---|---|
| Open the library. Click the three tabs: Sound Books, Blending Books, Storybooks. Filter by level. | "Here is the full programme. 118 books in total: 73 Sound Books, 12 Blending Books and 33 Storybooks, across all eight levels. Filter by level and you see exactly what a child at that stage reads, with the focus sounds on every cover. A teacher can pull the right book for the right group in seconds." |

### 4:15-5:00 — Curriculum mapping

| On screen | What you say |
|---|---|
| Open the curriculum mapping page. | "If you are an international school, this matters. The eight levels are mapped to the UK National Curriculum, Australian, US Common Core and IB PYP. So whatever framework you report against, you can show where each level sits. Letters and Sounds underneath, your local curriculum on top." |

### 5:00-5:45 — Close

| On screen | What you say |
|---|---|
| Back to the dashboard. Webcam to camera if you have one. | "So that is the whole loop. Assess every child honestly. See the whole school on one screen. Give each reader a book where every word is decodable at their level. And track who is moving and who needs you. It was built by a practising British primary teacher, not a software company, which is why it follows how phonics is actually taught." |
| Lower third: your email or booking link. | "If you would like this set up for your school, with your classes and your pupils loaded in, reply to this and I will get you a trial. Thanks for watching." |

### Production notes

- Record at 1080p minimum. Hide your bookmarks bar and any personal tabs.
- Use the seeded demo school with realistic names and a believable level spread. Empty screens kill the pitch.
- Keep the cursor calm. Move, pause, then talk. Do not narrate every click.
- If a feature is still "coming soon" in the build, do not show it. Only demo what works cleanly.
- Aim for one clean take per section, then cut together. Easier than one perfect 6-minute run.

## SCRIPT 3: Micro-clips (4 x ~30s)

Single-feature cuts. Each one stands alone for social or a follow-up email. Same facts, tighter.

### Clip A — The decodable rule

**[On screen: a Storybook page, sounds highlighted as you read.]**

> "Here is the one rule that changes everything. In every one of these books, every single word is decodable at the child's level. No surprise words. No guessing. The child meets a word, sounds it out, and gets it right. That is what builds a confident reader, and it is why these books work where generic readers do not."

### Clip B — One dashboard, whole school

**[On screen: the dashboard stats row and level-distribution bar.]**

> "This is every reader in your school on one screen. Who is assessed, who is not, and exactly which level each child is working at. The panel even flags children who have been stuck on the same level for a month. A phonics lead can see in ten seconds what used to take an afternoon with a spreadsheet."

### Clip C — Honest assessment

**[On screen: the assessment result screen.]**

> "Most reading assessments tell you a level and hope you trust it. This one separates what a child has actually passed from what was never tested. So when it places a child at level four, you know what that means, and so does the parent. Honest placement is the whole foundation, so we made it honest first."

### Clip D — The three-book pathway

**[On screen: Sound Book, Blending Book, Storybook in sequence.]**

> "Every level follows the same three steps. A Sound Book to introduce the new sounds. A Blending Book to practise joining them. Then a Storybook to read for real, with every word decodable. Introduce, practise, apply. The teaching sequence is built into the books, so a non-specialist can deliver it well."
