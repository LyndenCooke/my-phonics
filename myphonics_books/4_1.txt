   For Grown-Ups

      Your child reads this book, you don't read it to them. Every word is decodable
      using sounds taught up to Level 4, plus a small set of tricky words. Stay close,
      sound out together when stuck, and keep reading sessions short and warm.

      WHILE READING
          Stuck on a word? Try: "Sound it out: /ur/... /a/... /t/. Now blend it:
          sat."
          Going too fast? Slow them with: "Point to each word as you say it."
          Finger-tracking builds one-to-one matching.
          Tricky words? Don't try to sound them out. Tell them the word straight
          away: "That one says the."

      AFTER READING
          Try the activities at the back. They reinforce the focus sounds, sight
          words, and comprehension.
          Read this book again another day. Repeated reading is the strongest
          fluency-builder we know.
          Praise effort, not cleverness: "I love how hard you tried to sound that
          out" beats "You're so clever."

            Children become fluent readers by reading the same decodable book
            three to five times -- not by reading many books once. Plan for re-reads.

Build a Word

Say the word. Listen for each sound. Write one letter or sound in each box.

purple  purse

turned  ferns

Grown-up tip: Don't spell the word for them. Say it slowly, stretching each
sound: fff -- iii -- sh. Let them write each sound in its own box. Digraphs (two
letters making one sound) sit in one box together.
