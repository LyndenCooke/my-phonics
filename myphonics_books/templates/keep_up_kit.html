The Keep-Up Kit

Teacher Guide
Keep-up, not catch-up
{% for L in levels %}{% endfor %}

In an effective phonics programme, no child is left to fall behind and "catch up later" — the moment a sound is not secure, they get a little extra help before the class moves on. The Keep-Up Kit is that help: a 10-minute daily one-to-one routine, run with resources your school already has, for any child who needs it. Ten minutes a day, one focus sound at a time, until it sticks. The gap never gets the chance to grow.

Who is it for?
Any child not secure at the Day-5 "Prove it" check.
  • They could not read the week's sound quickly, or
  • they could not blend it in words, or
  • they read the words but stumbled on the decodable book.

It is not an intervention group, a label, or a "bottom table". It is a short, warm, private top-up — most children need it for only a week or two, a few times a year.

Who runs it, and when?
Any trained adult: teacher, teaching assistant, or a consistent volunteer.
  • Daily — the routine works through frequency, not length.
  • 10 minutes, always the same five steps (page 2).
  • Best before the next whole-class session, so the child walks in already warmed up.
  • One focus sound at a time. Never stack several.
What's in the kit? (all existing scheme resources — nothing new to buy)
  • This guide — the routine (page 2), a photocopiable routine card (page 3) and a two-week keep-up log (page 4).
  • Sound cards — the free-printable tier of the whole-programme deck, for the speed review and the focus sound.
  • Picture-word cards — 169 double-sided cards covering Levels 1–2, anchoring each early sound to a picture and a word.
  • Word cards — one deck per level (plain front, marked back), for blending five words a day.
  • Single-sound worksheets — one sheet per sound (Levels 1–3), for the "write it" step and independent follow-up.
  • The child's decodable storybook — the same book the class is reading, for the one-sentence read.

Pages 5–6 map every level to the exact resources to pull.

The principle: keep up, not catch up

Catch-up starts after a gap has formed and has to close it. Keep-up starts the day the Day-5 check flags a sound, so there is never a gap to close. A child on the routine stays with their class, keeps their book, and keeps their confidence — they simply get ten extra minutes until the focus sound is secure on two days in a row. Then the routine stops.

What does "secure" look like?
On a secure day the child, without help:
  • says the focus sound within two seconds of seeing the card,
  • blends at least 4 of the 5 words first time,
  • writes both words with the focus sound spelt correctly,
  • reads the sentence without stopping at the focus word.
Tick the log (page 4) with a ✓ on a secure day, a · on a not-yet day.
When to stop — and when to step back
Stop after 2 consecutive secure days (two ✓s in a row on the log). Record the secure date and return to normal practice.

Still not secure after 10 sessions? The focus sound probably is not the real gap. Re-run the level's Quick Level Check to find the earlier sound that is wobbling, and restart the routine there. Tell the class teacher — never simply keep going.
