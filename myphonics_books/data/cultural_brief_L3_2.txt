CULTURAL BRIEF: The Stone Flute
Region: Morocco (Casablanca)
Setting: Modern family apartment in Casablanca, Morocco

CONTEMPORARY REALITY CHECK:
- What does a normal day look like for a child here RIGHT NOW?
  A child in Casablanca goes to a modern school, comes home to an apartment, does homework,
  plays with siblings, watches TV or plays games. Weekends might involve visiting the corniche
  (waterfront), playing at Arab League Park, or going to Morocco Mall. Very similar to a
  British child's routine.
- What would a visitor see walking down an ordinary street?
  Mix of modern apartment blocks, some French colonial Art Deco architecture, cars, motorbikes,
  palm trees, small shops and cafes, modern signage in Arabic and French. NOT a souk or desert.
- Is this setting contemporary-first? YES — modern apartment interior with Moroccan decorative texture.

CLOTHING:
- Child (everyday): Bright t-shirt (coral/pink), dark leggings, trainers. Natural curly dark
  brown hair, possibly in a ponytail or loose. Olive/brown skin.
- Adult (Mum): Comfortable modern clothes — a loose tunic top over trousers, or a casual house
  caftan. Mix of modern and traditional is authentically Moroccan.
- Adult (Dad): Casual modern — t-shirt/polo and trousers. May wear a simple djellaba at home
  for comfort (this is genuinely common for relaxation).
- Modesty note: Full coverage. Leggings under any dress/tunic. No bare legs.

ARCHITECTURE/INTERIOR:
- Modern reality: Apartment with tiled floors (zellige-inspired geometric tiles in blue/white
  or green/white), whitewashed or cream painted walls, arched doorways (common Moroccan
  architectural feature even in modern buildings), modern kitchen visible.
- Cultural texture: Carved wooden shelf or cabinet, brass tray on a low table, colourful
  cushions on a low salon sofa (salon marocain), maybe a small tagine pot visible, patterned
  rug. Natural light through arched or rectangular windows.
- NOT tourist-riad style — this is a normal family home with Moroccan touches.

LANDSCAPE/ENVIRONMENT:
- If visible from windows: Casablanca skyline with white/cream buildings, palm trees,
  satellite dishes on rooftops (very common), blue sky.
- Mediterranean climate: warm, sunny, bright light.
- Plants: potted plants on balcony — bougainvillea, mint in pots (mint tea culture).

FOOD (if relevant):
- Not central to this story. If background detail needed: mint tea glasses, a plate of
  msemen (flatbread) or fruit on the table.

CULTURAL NOTES:
- Family warmth is central — Moroccan families are close, affectionate, physically near
  each other (sitting close, sharing space).
- Music is important in Moroccan culture — Gnawa, Andalusian, Amazigh traditions. A stone
  flute/ocarina fits the cultural connection to handmade musical instruments.
- Family terms: Mum and Dad used in English text (the book is in English).
- The flute could be something passed down — family heirloom feel — which is culturally
  authentic (Moroccan families treasure inherited objects).

STEREOTYPE WARNINGS:
- Do NOT show: desert, camels, souks as the ONLY setting
- Do NOT dress the child in traditional djellaba/kaftan for everyday at-home play
- Do NOT show poverty or run-down interiors
- Do show: modern apartment life with warm Moroccan decorative touches
- Do show: family warmth, music, beauty of everyday life

CONTEMPORARY BALANCE VERDICT:
- Would a child FROM this culture recognise their daily life? YES — modern apartment,
  normal family, familiar objects with Moroccan character.
- Are we showing the culture as it IS, not as the West imagines it? YES — not a souk or
  desert scene. A real home.
- Does tradition appear as seasoning within modern life? YES — zellige tiles, arched
  doorways, cushions, brass tray — within a modern apartment.

DIGNITY CHECK:
- Would someone from Morocco feel proud seeing this? YES — shows a comfortable, warm,
  loving family in a beautiful modern Moroccan home.
- Does this feel like a REAL place where REAL people live well? YES.
