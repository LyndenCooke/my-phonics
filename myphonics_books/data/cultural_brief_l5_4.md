# CULTURAL BRIEF: A Place for Me (L5.4)

**Region:** Colombian Caribbean coast — Cartagena, Bolivar Department
**Setting:** Morning fruit market in the walled old city (Centro Historico), NOT the Bazurto market (which is chaotic and outside the old city). Think the smaller, calmer fruit stalls and vendors within the cobblestone streets of the walled city itself.

## CONTEMPORARY REALITY CHECK

- **Normal day for a child here:** Hot tropical weather (30-33C). Kids wear light casual clothes — t-shirts, shorts or light trousers, sandals or trainers. School uniforms during the week. After school: play outside, family time, fruit juices from street vendors.
- **Walking down an ordinary street in the walled city:** Narrow cobblestone streets flanked by colourful colonial buildings (2-3 storeys) with large ornate wooden doors, iron balconies overflowing with bougainvillea in pink, purple, orange. Palm trees. Warm golden light. Mix of tourists and locals. Fruit vendors with carts. Modern shops alongside historic architecture.
- **Contemporary-first?** YES — this is a living, modern city with colonial architecture as its genuine streetscape. The colonial buildings ARE the contemporary reality — people live and work in them today.

## ARCHITECTURE — Cartagena Walled City

- **Buildings:** Spanish colonial style, 2-3 storeys. Thick plastered walls painted in VIVID colours: bright yellow (the most common), terracotta orange, cobalt blue, white, coral pink, mint green. NOT pastel — Cartagena's colours are BOLD and saturated.
- **Balconies:** Wooden balconies with iron railings, overflowing with bougainvillea, ferns, and trailing plants. This is THE signature look of Cartagena.
- **Doors:** Large, heavy wooden doors — often dark brown or painted in contrasting colours. Ornate metal door knockers.
- **Streets:** Narrow cobblestone (adoquin) streets. Stone or brick.
- **Roofs:** Terracotta clay tile roofs (warm reddish-brown).

## VEGETATION — What ACTUALLY Grows Here

**CRITICAL — Use these, NOT generic "tropical trees":**
- **Bougainvillea:** THE most common plant. Cascading from every balcony. Colours: hot pink, magenta, purple, orange, white. This is Cartagena's signature plant.
- **Palm trees:** Coconut palms (tall, curved trunks, feathery fronds) and fan palms. NOT the generic symmetrical cartoon palm — real coconut palms lean and curve.
- **Plumeria (frangipani):** White and yellow flowers, common in gardens and on streets.
- **Tropical flowering vines:** Trailing from balconies and walls.
- **NO cherry trees, NO temperate trees, NO pine, NO birch, NO oak.**

## MARKET PRODUCE — What's Actually Sold

**Tropical fruit (the stars of a Cartagena fruit stall):**
- Mangoes (multiple varieties — Azucar, Hilacha, Vallento — bright yellow-orange-red)
- Papaya (large, green-skinned with orange flesh)
- Guanabana/soursop (large, green, spiky-looking)
- Bananas and plantains (green cooking plantains AND yellow sweet bananas)
- Lulo (small, orange, fuzzy)
- Maracuya/passion fruit (round, purple or yellow)
- Coconuts (green, sold for drinking)
- Watermelon
- Pineapple

**Other produce:**
- Avocados (large Colombian variety)
- Limes (small, green)
- Peppers and chillies
- Yuca/cassava

**Market stall style:** Simple wooden tables or carts, often with a canvas or fabric canopy for shade. Fruit displayed in colourful piles and baskets. Some vendors use wheelbarrows or pushcarts.

## CLOTHING

- **Local boy (6yo, everyday):** Light casual clothes — a colourful t-shirt, light trousers or shorts (shorts are genuinely common in this heat). Sandals or trainers. For our book: bright green t-shirt, dark blue long trousers (modesty), trainers.
- **Visiting British boy (6yo):** Blue t-shirt, khaki trousers, white trainers. Would look paler and more "tourist" compared to locals.
- **Adults at market:** Mix of casual modern clothes. Vendors might wear aprons. Women in light dresses or blouses with trousers. Men in t-shirts and trousers.
- **Grandmother (vendor):** Colourful blouse or dress, possibly a head wrap (turbante) — Afro-Colombian women on the Caribbean coast often wear colourful turbantes as a cultural tradition. Long skirt or trousers. Apron if working the stall.

## CULTURAL NOTES

- **Skin tones:** Cartagena is ethnically diverse — significant Afro-Colombian population (especially in neighbourhoods like Getsemani), mestizo, and white Colombian. The local boy could be any of these. For visual distinction from the British visitor, medium-warm brown skin works.
- **Family terms:** Grandmother = "abuela" or "abuelita" (we won't use Spanish in the text, but the illustration should show a warm grandmother figure)
- **Market atmosphere:** Friendly, social, community-oriented. Vendors know their regular customers. It's a social gathering place, not just shopping.
- **Sounds:** Birds, street vendors calling out, conversation, maybe distant music from a radio (vallenato or cumbia).

## STEREOTYPE WARNINGS

- **AVOID:** Generic "Latin fiesta" imagery — no sombreros, no maracas, no carnival costumes
- **AVOID:** Poverty imagery — Cartagena's walled city is well-maintained and beautiful
- **AVOID:** Generic "tropical paradise" — show the SPECIFIC character of Cartagena (colonial buildings, wooden doors, bougainvillea balconies)
- **AVOID:** Making all background people dark-skinned — Cartagena is diverse
- **DO SHOW:** The genuine warmth and colour of Cartagena — vivid building colours, overflowing balconies, tropical fruit, cobblestone streets

## DIGNITY CHECK

- Would a Colombian from Cartagena feel proud seeing this? YES — it shows their city as beautiful, welcoming, and vibrant
- Does it feel like a real place? YES — specific architectural details, specific vegetation, specific produce
- Is tradition seasoning within modern life? YES — the colonial architecture IS the modern reality, the market IS contemporary daily life
