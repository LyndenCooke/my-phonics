CULTURAL BRIEF: L3.3 — Reach for the Treat!
Region: Greater Accra, Ghana (West Africa)
Setting: A residential neighbourhood yard/compound with a mango tree

CONTEMPORARY REALITY CHECK:
- Normal day for a child: School in uniform, then home to change into casual clothes.
  Play football in the yard or street, help with chores, play with neighbours' kids.
  Community is tight — neighbours know each other, kids play together freely.
- Ordinary street: Painted concrete houses (cream, pastel blue, terracotta, white),
  some with low compound walls. Trotros (shared minibuses) on main roads.
  Mango trees, palm trees, bougainvillea. Warm, bright sunshine.
- Is this setting contemporary-first? YES — modern residential neighbourhood.

CLOTHING:
- Boy (everyday): Bright cotton t-shirt (e.g., yellow or green), knee-length shorts,
  sandals or trainers. Light and breathable for tropical heat.
- Girl (everyday): Cotton dress with bold print (not necessarily kente — could be
  any bright African-inspired or plain pattern), leggings underneath for modesty,
  sandals or trainers.
- Traditional occasions only: Kente cloth or ankara print outfits for church,
  naming ceremonies, cultural days at school. NOT everyday wear for children.
- Adults: Mix of modern casual and African print. Women may wear modern clothes
  or colourful print dresses. Men in polo shirts, trousers.
- Modesty note: Girl wears dress with leggings underneath. Boy in shorts to knees.

ARCHITECTURE/INTERIOR:
- Modern reality: Single or two-storey painted concrete/plastered houses.
  Colours: cream, white, pastel blue, terracotta, yellow. Low compound walls
  with metal gates. Flat or corrugated metal roofs.
- Cultural texture: Some houses have decorative blocks or patterned railings.
  Bright plastic chairs on verandas. Open-air cooking areas common.
- Yards/compounds: Shared outdoor space. Swept red earth or concrete.
  Trees providing shade (mango, neem, palm).

LANDSCAPE/ENVIRONMENT:
- Lush tropical vegetation: mango trees (very common), coconut palms,
  banana plants, bougainvillea (pink/purple), hibiscus
- Warm bright sunshine, vivid blue sky
- Red/laterite earth visible in unpaved areas
- Animals: chickens roaming, lizards on walls (background detail only)

FOOD (relevant to story):
- Mangoes are abundant and beloved. Children pick them from trees.
  Ripe mangoes are golden-orange, sweet, juicy.
- A mango is the "treat" in the story — culturally authentic.

CULTURAL NOTES:
- Family terms: "Nana" = grandparent (Twi). Very common and respectful.
- Community child-rearing: neighbours look out for all children
- Children play together freely across households — making friends with
  a stranger child in the neighbourhood is completely natural
- Football (soccer) is hugely popular
- Twi is widely spoken alongside English

STEREOTYPE WARNINGS:
- AVOID: Poverty imagery, slums, mud huts, safari animals, "Africa" as one place
- AVOID: Only showing traditional dress — kids wear modern casual clothes
- SHOW: Modern painted houses, lush tropical setting, happy community life
- SHOW: Children as resourceful, creative, active agents
- Mango tree is NOT exotic tourism — it is genuine everyday life

CONTEMPORARY BALANCE VERDICT:
- Would a child from Accra recognise their daily life? YES
- Are we showing the culture as it IS? YES — modern neighbourhood with tropical character
- Does tradition appear as seasoning, not the whole meal? YES — kente accents possible
  on cushions/cloth in background, but setting is modern residential

DIGNITY CHECK:
- Would a Ghanaian person feel proud seeing this? YES — warm, vibrant, modern
  neighbourhood with beautiful tropical setting and resourceful children
- Does this feel like a REAL place? YES — painted concrete houses, mango trees,
  community life, red earth, bright sunshine
