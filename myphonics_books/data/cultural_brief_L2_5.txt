CULTURAL BRIEF: Book 2.5 (The Loud Boy / The Proud Boy)
Region: Mombasa, Kenya (East Africa)
Setting: A lively coastal neighbourhood with modern elements and Swahili architectural touches.

CONTEMPORARY REALITY CHECK:
- What does a normal day look like for a child here RIGHT NOW? Walking through busy streets with a mix of modern and historic buildings, visiting local markets for fresh fruit, playing outdoors, riding in tuk-tuks or cars.
- What would a visitor see walking down an ordinary street? Paved roads, matatus (minibuses) and tuk-tuks, colourful market stalls selling fruit (coconuts, mangoes) and snacks (mandazi), modern shops alongside older Swahili-influenced buildings with wooden balconies.
- Is this setting contemporary-first? YES. We are showing everyday modern life in a bustling Kenyan coastal city, not a rural safari stereotype.

CLOTHING:
- Child (everyday): Modern casual, suitable for a warm climate. A bright t-shirt (e.g., yellow or patterned) and comfortable trousers or long shorts that cover the knees.
- Adult(s): Men in collared shirts and trousers or jeans; women in colourful printed dresses (Kanga/Kitenge patterns), sometimes with a light headscarf.
- Modesty note: Coastal Kenya has a significant Muslim population and generally conservative dress. Trousers/jeans or dresses with leggings are preferred. Shoulders and knees should be covered.

ARCHITECTURE/INTERIOR:
- Modern reality: Concrete and whitewashed stone buildings, paved streets, glass shopfronts.
- Cultural texture: Intricately carved wooden doors (typical of Swahili architecture in Mombasa's Old Town), arched windows, vibrant painted walls, bougainvillea flowers spilling over walls.
- Typical objects: colourful tuk-tuk on the street, woven baskets at a fruit stall.

LANDSCAPE/ENVIRONMENT:
- Bright, sunny, tropical coastal weather.
- Palm trees (coconut palms), bougainvillea vines, Indian Ocean visible in the distance or at the end of the street.
- Stray cats are common in Mombasa streets.

FOOD (if relevant):
- Coconuts, mangoes, pineapples, mandazi (fried dough), samosas.

CULTURAL NOTES:
- Swahili greetings ("Jambo" / "Habari")
- Strong sense of community and street life.

STEREOTYPE WARNINGS:
- AVOID "safari" imagery (no lions, giraffes, or mud huts in the city).
- AVOID depicting everyone in extreme poverty; show a vibrant, functioning, middle-class or working-class neighbourhood.
- AVOID rural Maasai warriors; this is a contemporary urban coastal setting.

CONTEMPORARY BALANCE VERDICT:
- Would a child FROM this culture recognise their daily life in this book? YES.
- Are we showing the culture as it IS, not as the West imagines it? YES.
- Does tradition appear as seasoning within modern life, not as the entire meal? YES.

DIGNITY CHECK:
- Would someone from Mombasa feel proud seeing this? YES, it celebrates the vibrant, sunny, and unique architectural blend of their city without relying on tired tropes.
- Does this feel like a REAL place where REAL people live well? YES.

SOURCES:
- Contemporary local news and travel vlog observations of Mombasa streets (Ganjoni, Old Town).
